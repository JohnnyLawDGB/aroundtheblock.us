This page describes capabilities. It is not a list of case outcomes, not a certification, and not a promise of recovery or security.

On-chain investigations and theft tracing

When DigiByte (DGB) is stolen or sent to a fraudulent site, the blockchain keeps a permanent public record of where it went. We follow that public record and write down only what it proves.

Tracing DigiByte thefts is free public research, not a paid investigative service. We never promise recovery. Most stolen crypto is not recovered. The value of the work is a clear, checkable trace and honest limits.

How the work is bounded:

  • Funds are traced hop by hop from a confirmed on-chain anchor.
  • Every published figure carries a label for how solid it is (for example Certified, Floor, Sampled, or Contaminated). Sampled or contaminated numbers are never presented as a theft total.
  • Facts anyone can check stay separate from observations that are not proven.
  • Intent is never claimed. The chain shows what moved, not why.
  • Victim names, transaction IDs, wallet addresses, amounts, and dates are not published in a way that identifies a victim.
  • Nothing goes public until it has been checked against the chain and reviewed by a person.
  • We never ask for a seed phrase, private keys, passwords, or remote access to a device. Do not send them.

Website security review (free for DigiByte builders)

A website security review is a point-in-time look at a public website, with human-reviewed findings and suggested fixes.

If you publicly build or maintain a DigiByte project, your review is free. Free reviews get the same care and checks as any other review. Each one covers the site you name, within the scope agreed in writing.

What a default review is:

  • AI-assisted tools look at what a normal visitor can already see.
  • A person reviews every finding before it is shared.
  • It is a snapshot of the site on the day it is tested.
  • It is not monitoring, not a certification, and not a guarantee that the site is secure.

How work starts (none of this starts from a draft alone):

  1. Prepare a request. The intake below is copy and field specification only. It does not send a message by itself and does not start a review.
  2. Confirm control of the site with a DNS record or a small file we give you.
  3. Agree on scope in writing before any work. Default testing looks only at what a normal visitor can see unless a separate signed authorization covers deeper testing.
  4. Receive findings privately, by a channel agreed for that project.

Preparing a request is not permission to test. Nothing starts until control and written scope are confirmed. We never ask for passwords, seed phrases, private keys, payment details, or credentials. Do not send them.

Findings are shared privately by a channel agreed for that project. We do not publish them without written consent. Secrets are redacted in the report.

This page does not claim DigiScope-account delivery. Live site-review copy (as of Nov 8, 2026) uses an agreed private channel, not a DigiScope portal. DigiScope private report delivery is omitted here until separately verified as working.

Optional badge after a review (if requested): Reviewed by AroundTheBlock.Us on the review date. It says reviewed, never secured. It is not a certification. It links to a verification page with review date, expiry, and a plain disclaimer. It expires 90 days after the review unless renewed by a re-review. It can be revoked if misused.

What is not included:

  • Continuous monitoring, firewalls, malware cleanup, or incident response.
  • Any promise that a site is secure or free of problems.
  • Fixing the issues (findings and guidance only, unless separately agreed).
  • Anything that changes data, denial of service, or social engineering.
  • Host, CDN, or cloud provider systems unless that provider's permission is obtained first.

Fine print

  1. Default services are point-in-time testing of public-facing websites and related public surfaces using current AI-assisted toolkits, operated and supervised by a person. Methods may change. Default testing is passive and non-intrusive.
  2. Deeper, targeted, or active testing happens only with a written request and a signed authorization and rules of engagement that name assets, methods, time window, rate limits, and contacts.
  3. Each review reflects only what was observed on the date or dates tested. Not continuous monitoring or incident response.
  4. Services, reports, and any badge are provided as is. No warranty or guarantee that any site is secure, free of defects, or free of vulnerabilities. Findings are advisory only.
  5. Client must confirm ownership or owner authorization in writing. Control is verified before testing. Third-party infrastructure needs that provider's permission when required.
  6. Findings and engagement data are treated as confidential, used only to perform the services, and not published without written consent except as required by law. Secrets are redacted in reports.
  7. A person reviews findings before delivery. AI-assisted output can contain errors.
