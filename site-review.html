Website Security Review

By Mark McNiel (JohnnyLaw), Around The Block LLC, Stillwater, Oklahoma. Last updated Nov 5, 2026.

I look at your website the way an outsider would. I use the latest available security toolkits to test your public surfaces, then a person goes through every result, removes the noise, and writes up what matters in plain language. If you want deeper, targeted scans, I can do those on request once we agree in writing on exactly what is in scope.

This is a snapshot of your site on the day it was tested. It is not monitoring, and it is not a guarantee that your site is secure.

  • Your report is delivered privately in your own DigiScope account. You never share a password with me.

How it works

  1. Tell me about your site. You fill out a short intake and confirm in writing that you own the site or are allowed to have it tested.
  2. Prove you control the domain. You add a DNS TXT record or a small file at a /.well-known path I give you. No testing starts until that checks out.
  3. I run the review. Current toolkits test your public surfaces under human supervision.
  4. A person checks the results. False alarms come out. Real issues get a plain explanation and suggested fixes.
  5. You get your report privately. It appears in your DigiScope account portal.

How your report is delivered

Reports go into the DigiScope account portal that belongs to you. You sign in to your own account to read and download it. I never ask for your password, and you should never send it to me or anyone else. Findings stay confidential and are not shared without your written consent. Secrets are redacted in the report.

The badge

If you want, you can show a dated badge on your site after a review:

Reviewed by AroundTheBlock.Us on [date]

  • It says reviewed, never secured. It is not a certification.
  • It links to a verification page with the review date, expiry date, and a plain disclaimer.
  • It expires 90 days after the review. A re-review renews it.
  • I can revoke it if it is misused, and it comes down when it expires.

What is not included

  • Continuous monitoring, firewalls, malware cleanup, or incident response.
  • Any promise that your site is secure or free of problems.
  • Fixing the issues. You get findings and guidance; the fixes are yours unless we agree otherwise.
  • Anything that changes your data, denial of service, or social engineering.
  • Your host, CDN, or cloud provider, unless you get their permission first.
